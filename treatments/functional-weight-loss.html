How does medical weight loss work?

GLO's Functional Weight Loss program starts with comprehensive metabolic labs to find what is driving your weight. Your provider then builds a plan that can include FDA-approved GLP-1 medications such as semaglutide or tirzepatide when appropriate, plus nutrition and lifestyle guidance. GLP-1 medications mimic a natural gut hormone that helps regulate appetite and blood sugar, so you feel full sooner. Doses are increased gradually, with regular follow-ups and labs to adjust your plan.

Is medical weight loss right for me?

A good fit if…

  • ✓You've tried diet and exercise and your weight won't budge
  • ✓You have signs of insulin resistance, pre-diabetes or hormone-related weight gain
  • ✓You want a full program with labs, nutrition and monitoring, not just a prescription
  • ✓You're ready for gradual, sustainable change rather than a quick fix

Talk to us first if…

  • –You're pregnant, breastfeeding or planning a pregnancy soon
  • –You or a family member have had medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2 — GLP-1 medications are not recommended
  • –You have a history of pancreatitis or certain gastrointestinal conditions — your provider will review alternatives
  • –Your main concern is fatigue or low libido from hormone changes — hormone replacement therapy may be the better fit

You start with metabolic testing, then get a personalized plan. If a GLP-1 medication is prescribed, you take a weekly injection with gradual dose increases and regular check-ins and lab work.

Medical Weight Loss FAQs

Do you prescribe FDA-approved medications?

Yes — when appropriate, we prescribe FDA-approved GLP-1 medications like semaglutide and tirzepatide (brand options include Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound, and Mounjaro). We're transparent about the difference between FDA-approved and compounded versions.

How much weight can I lose with medical weight loss?

Clinical trials show average losses in the range of 15–20%+ of body weight over about 68–72 weeks at full maintenance dose for GLP-1 medications, though individual results vary and depend on your full program, not medication alone.

Is medical weight loss just a prescription, or a full program?

It's a full program — metabolic labs, personalized nutrition, and ongoing monitoring, not just a medication refill.

What are the side effects of GLP-1 medications?

The most common side effects are nausea, constipation, diarrhea and reduced appetite, which often ease as your body adjusts. Doses are increased gradually to help, and your provider will monitor you and adjust your plan as needed.

How long do I need to stay on a weight loss program?

It depends on your goals and how your body responds. Functional weight loss is an ongoing program, and many people see meaningful results by 8–12 weeks. Your provider will discuss maintenance and next steps as you progress.

Semaglutide vs tirzepatide: what's the difference?

Both are weekly GLP-1-based injections. Semaglutide (Wegovy, Ozempic) acts on the GLP-1 receptor; tirzepatide (Zepbound, Mounjaro) acts on both GLP-1 and GIP receptors. Your provider will recommend the option that fits your labs, health history and goals.
